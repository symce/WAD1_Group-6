About the Website

Safeguarding Indigenous Weaving Traditions & Cultural Ethics

Indigenous textiles are living historical documents that carry ancestral memory, ecological knowledge of botanical dyes, and sacred spiritual prayers. Preserving these traditions protects endangered cultural identities from commercial misappropriation and extinction.

Three Craft Mediums

  • 1. Textiles: Backstrap and pedal-loom fabrics woven from fine abaca, hand-spun native cotton, and silk using warp-ikat and tapestry techniques.
  • 2. Bark Cloth: Ancient non-woven garments (Tinukpuk and Akfè) crafted by harvesting and beating Ficus and Artocarpus inner tree bark.
  • 3. Plaited Fiber: Sacred ritual basketry and sleeping mats (Tingkop, Tepoh, and Gabe) hand-plaited from buri, pandan, and smoked bamboo.

Geographical & Cultural Scope

  • Luzon: Ifugao, Ibaloi, Bugkalot/Ilongot, Agta/Dumagat, Balangao, Kalinga, Bontoc, and Gaddang weavers.
  • Visayas & Mindoro: Hanunó'o Mangyan, Suludnon (Panay Bukidnon), Tagbanwa & Palaw'an, and Magahat/Karol-an communities.
  • Mindanao & Sulu: T'boli, Yakan, Maranao, Mandaya, Subanen, Teduray, Banwaon, Erumanen Manobo, B'laan, Bagobo Tagabawa, Tausūg, and Samal/Badjao.

Cultural Ethics & IP Protection

Guided by the Indigenous Peoples' Rights Act (RA 8371), this archive respects community ownership of traditional motifs and cultural knowledge.

Member submissions are held in a strict PENDING state until an Admin verifies documentation sources and Free and Prior Informed Consent (FPIC).
